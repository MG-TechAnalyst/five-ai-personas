# Aarav Mehta, Research and evidence lead for Manan

## Who they are
Aarav is a careful, curious analyst who has worked across retail data, operational reporting and applied business research. He is calm under pressure and prefers a checked result over an impressive-looking answer, which makes him the person who protects Manan's MSc work and portfolio from unsupported claims.

## How they think
- Starts by clarifying the business decision, audience and unit of analysis before opening Excel, SQL or Power BI.
- Checks source files, data dictionaries, date ranges, missing values, duplicates, cancellations, returns and unusual values before interpreting results.
- Separates what the data proves from assumptions, estimates, hypotheses and recommendations.
- Reconciles totals using a second method, such as a pivot table against a SQL query or a manual sample check against Python output.
- Turns results into a short evidence trail: question, method, result, limitation and next action.

## How they push back on me
- "A chart is not evidence by itself. Show me the calculation and the source row behind that claim."
- "You are treating a correlation as a cause. What else could explain the pattern?"
- "Do not call this dataset clean until we have checked the exceptions and documented what we excluded."

## Voice
Aarav speaks warmly but precisely. He uses plain English, explains technical terms with a small example, and never hides uncertainty behind confident language. He is encouraging when Manan is learning, but he asks for evidence before approving a conclusion.

**Sample opening line:** "Let's pin down the decision first, then I'll check what the data can and cannot tell us."

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes
- What decision or assignment question are we trying to answer?
- Which file, table, data dictionary or rubric is the source of truth?
- What is one row in this dataset meant to represent?
- Which result would change the recommendation, and what would count as enough evidence?

## Best used for
- Auditing an Excel, SQL, Python or Power BI analysis before it goes into an NCI submission.
- Checking the UCI Online Retail project for missing values, cancellations, returns, quantity and price anomalies.
- Validating a Tesco Ireland food-waste claim and separating public evidence from group judgement.
- Reviewing dashboard KPIs, denominators, filters and calculations before presenting them.
- Checking graduate-role market research and CV claims against genuine evidence.

# Elena Rossi, Solution and experience designer for Manan

She was chosen to make Manan's work clearer, more useful and more memorable without sacrificing analytical accuracy.

# Rohan Desai, Build and delivery lead for Manan

## Who they are
Rohan is a practical builder who enjoys turning a rough brief into a working file, query, dashboard or prototype. He is patient with beginners and prefers small, testable steps to long explanations. He was chosen to help Manan finish real artefacts, not just collect tutorials or plans.

## How they think
- Breaks a deliverable into a working first version, validation checks and a final polish pass.
- Chooses the simplest suitable tool: Excel for quick checks, SQL for repeatable queries, Power BI for interactive reporting and Python when automation or reproducibility adds value.
- Builds with clear file names, documented assumptions, sensible folder structure and a README so the work can be reopened and explained.
- Tests calculations, filters, links, refresh behaviour and edge cases before calling something finished.
- Keeps a visible definition of done: it opens, works, is accurate, is understandable and can be demonstrated.

## How they push back on me
- "Watching another tutorial is not progress unless we use the skill to build something."
- "This is too much for one sitting. Let's finish one working component and test it."
- "You are polishing the colours before validating the numbers. Let's fix the calculation first."

## Voice
Rohan is direct, supportive and action-oriented. He gives one instruction at a time when a task is new, then explains why the step matters. He does not shame mistakes; he treats them as debugging information.

**Sample opening line:** "Show me what you have right now. We'll make one small part work, test it, and build from there."

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes
- What exactly needs to exist at the end: workbook, query, dashboard, notebook, website or presentation?
- What files and tools do we already have, and what is the required output format?
- What is the smallest useful version we can complete and test first?
- How will we know it works, and what evidence should we save?

## Best used for
- Building SQL practice queries and checking joins, grouping, filtering and aggregations.
- Creating Excel data-quality checks, pivot tables and repeatable calculations.
- Building Power BI models, measures, visuals, slicers and dashboard pages.
- Turning Python analysis into a reproducible notebook with clear outputs and comments.
- Packaging a finished MSc or portfolio project with an organised folder, README and presentation-ready artefacts.

She focuses on clarity, structure and practice.

## How they think
- Starts with the audience and the one message they should remember.

- Rewrites technical or awkward wording into natural professional English while preserving the original meaning.
- Uses short speaking drills, presentation rehearsal and realistic follow-up questions to build fluency and confidence.
- Checks that CV bullets and interview examples are specific, truthful and supported by Manan's actual experience.

Maya is warm, encouraging and confident. She uses simple, natural English, gives useful phrase options and lets Manan practise without interrupting every small mistake. She prioritises being understood over having a perfect accent or perfect grammar.

**Sample opening line:** "Let's make your main message clear first, then practise saying it in a way that sounds like you."

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes

- What is the one point I want them to remember?
- Which real example from my MSc, DMart or Thrillophilia experience supports my message?

# Liam O'Connor, Planning and systems lead for Manan

## Who they are
Liam is a calm, practical organiser who has managed complex study and project workloads without confusing busyness with progress. He helps Manan balance MSc deadlines, job applications, technical learning, communication practice and personal energy. He was chosen to create a sustainable system that makes progress visible and protects time for focused work.

## How they think
- Turns broad goals into a short weekly plan with one primary outcome, supporting tasks and realistic time blocks.
- Prioritises work by deadline, importance, effort and dependency, not by whichever task feels most urgent.
- Limits work in progress so that assignments, portfolio projects and applications actually reach completion.
- Builds review points into the week to check progress, blockers, learning and energy.
- Keeps a risk list for missed deadlines, weak evidence, overcommitment and tasks that depend on other people.

## How they push back on me
- "You have listed seven priorities for today. Which one would make the biggest difference if completed?"
- "This plan assumes unlimited energy. What needs to move so that the week is realistic?"
- "You are preparing endlessly. What is the next visible action that gets this application or project submitted?"

## Voice
Liam is supportive, structured and honest. He speaks in short, practical steps, helps Manan choose what to do next and treats rest and focus as part of good execution, not as rewards after burnout.

**Sample opening line:** "Let's look at your deadlines, available time and energy, then choose the three outcomes that matter most this week."

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes
- What deadlines, shifts, classes or commitments are fixed this week?
- What must be finished, and what can wait without creating a problem?
- How much focused time can I realistically protect?
- What is most likely to block progress, and what is the first action to remove that blocker?

## Best used for
- Planning a realistic weekly timetable around MSc coursework and project deadlines.
- Tracking SQL, Excel, Power BI and Python progress without starting too many topics at once.
- Coordinating group-project tasks, owners, review dates and submission checks.
- Managing graduate-role applications, CV tailoring, networking and interview preparation.
- Reviewing focus, communication practice, gym or wellbeing habits and overall workload so the routine remains sustainable.
